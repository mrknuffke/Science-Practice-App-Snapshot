02Level 2 synthesis · Main ideas

Part 1: Main Ideas Digest

Pillar 1: Newton's First Law & Free-Body Equilibrium

Forces describe physical pushes or pulls between bodies. They are measured in newtons (N).

  • Contact vs. Field Forces: Contact forces require physical touch (normal, friction, tension). Field forces act across distance without physical contact (gravity).
  • Newton's First Law: Objects maintain constant velocity unless acted upon by an unbalanced net external force.
  • Inertia and Mass: Mass is the quantitative measure of inertia. Greater mass means greater resistance to changes in motion.
  • Balanced Forces: Equilibrium requires the vector sum of all forces to equal zero (ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0).
  • Key Invariant: Constant velocity does not require any forward force. Motion continues indefinitely on its own unless friction or drag opposes it.

Pillar 2: Newton's Second Law

Newton's Second Law relates net force, mass, and acceleration (ΣF = ma).

  • Proportional Scaling: Acceleration is directly proportional to net force and inversely proportional to total mass.
  • Apparent Weight in Elevators: Normal force reflects apparent weight (FN = m(g + ay)). Upward acceleration increases apparent weight, downward acceleration decreases it, and free fall yields weightlessness.

Pillar 3: Newton's Third Law & Interaction Symmetry

Forces always occur in matched interaction pairs between two interacting bodies.

  • Interaction Equality: Whenever object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal and opposite force on object A.
  • Mathematical Symmetry: FA on B = −FB on A.
  • Four Pair Criteria: Action-reaction pairs satisfy four mandatory rules: equal magnitude, opposite direction, identical interaction type, and action on two distinct objects.
  • Why Pairs Never Cancel: Interaction pairs never cancel because they act on different bodies. Cancellation only occurs when opposing forces act on the same object.
  • Mass Invariance: Mass disparities do not change force equality. A heavy truck and a light fly experience identical contact forces during a collision.
  • Conservation of Momentum: For two bodies with no net outside force, the total momentum m1v1 + m2v2 stays the same.

Pillar 4: Coupled Systems

Connected bodies need careful boundary definitions.

  • Whole-System Method: Treat coupled bodies as a single entity to eliminate internal cord tensions.
  • Tension Isolation: Isolate an individual mass to solve for cord tension: ΣFisolated = misolated · a.

Components, Inclines and the Critical Angle

Extra depth. The rest of the review works without it.

  • Orthogonal Independence: Perpendicular dimensions act independently. Resolve forces into separate orthogonal axes: ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may.
  • Tilted Reference Frames: On an incline of angle θ, rotate coordinates parallel and perpendicular to the ramp surface.
  • Decomposed Gravity: Down-slope gravity component: Fg∥ = mg sinθ. Perpendicular component: Fg⊥ = mg cosθ.
  • Critical Slip Angle: An object begins sliding when tanθc = μs, independent of object mass.

Problem 1 · Equilibrium with two cablesEnrichment

A 40 kg environmental sensor pod hangs in equilibrium from two identical steel cables. Each cable makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal ceiling. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Write the equilibrium force equations for the horizontal (x) and vertical (y) directions.
  2. Calculate the tension force T in each support cable.
  3. Explain why cable tension increases drastically if the cables are pulled nearly horizontal.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Equilibrium equations: You write the horizontal equation: T1 cos 30° − T2 cos 30° = 0, so T1 = T2 = T.
  • You write the vertical equation: 2T sin 30° − mg = 0.
  • (b) Cable tension: You calculate 2T (0.50) = (40 kg)(9.8 m/s²) = 392 N, so T = 392 N in each cable.
  • (c) Angle analysis: You say that as the angle gets close to zero, sinθ gets close to zero.
  • You say T = mg / (2 sinθ) gets very large to support the weight.
Problem 2 · Newton's second law and apparent weight

A 60 kg specialist stands on a digital force plate inside a vertical mine shaft elevator. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. What force does the plate read when the elevator moves upward at a steady 5.0 m/s?
  2. What does the scale read when the elevator accelerates upward at +2.2 m/s²?
  3. What does the scale read when the elevator accelerates downward at −3.0 m/s²?
  4. What does the scale read if the elevator cable snaps and falls freely?
Scoring guide
  • (a) Constant velocity: You say zero acceleration means a zero net force, so FN = mg = (60)(9.8) = 588 N.
  • (b) Upward acceleration: You use the apparent weight: FN = m(g + a) = 60(9.8 + 2.2) = 60(12.0) = 720 N.
  • (c) Downward acceleration: You say it reduces the apparent weight: FN = m(g − a) = 60(9.8 − 3.0) = 60(6.8) = 408 N.
  • (d) Free fall: You say a = −g, so FN = m(g − g) = 0 N (apparent weightlessness).
Problem 3 · Newton's third law and deep-space recoil

An 80 kg astronaut floats at rest in deep space. The astronaut shoves a stationary 400 kg communications satellite with a forward force of 160 N for 0.50 seconds.

  1. What force does the satellite exert on the astronaut during the push? In which direction?
  2. Calculate the acceleration of the satellite and the acceleration of the astronaut during the shove.
  3. Calculate the final velocity of each body after the 0.50-second shove ends.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Interaction force: You say that by Newton's Third Law, the satellite pushes on the astronaut with exactly 160 N, in the opposite (backward) direction.
  • (b) Accelerations: You find the satellite: asat = 160 N / 400 kg = +0.40 m/s².
  • You find the astronaut: aastro = −160 N / 80 kg = −2.0 m/s².
  • (c) Final velocities: You find the satellite: vf = 0 + (0.40)(0.50) = +0.20 m/s.
  • You find the astronaut: vf = 0 + (−2.0)(0.50) = −1.0 m/s.

Problem 4 · Ramp dynamics and critical slipEnrichment

A 15 kg supply crate rests on a steel loading ramp inclined at 30°. The static friction coefficient is μs = 0.50, and the kinetic coefficient is μk = 0.30. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Calculate the gravity components parallel (Fg∥) and perpendicular (Fg⊥) to the ramp.
  2. Calculate the maximum static friction force. Does the crate slip or remain stationary?
  3. If pushed to start sliding, calculate the kinetic friction and the resulting down-slope acceleration.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Gravity components: You calculate Fg∥ = mg sin 30° = (15)(9.8)(0.5) = 73.5 N.
  • You calculate Fg⊥ = mg cos 30° = (15)(9.8)(0.866) = 127.3 N.
  • (b) Slip check: You find the maximum static friction: fs,max = μs FN = (0.50)(127.3 N) = 63.7 N.
  • You say the crate slips, because Fg∥ (73.5 N) is larger than fs,max.
  • (c) Sliding acceleration: You find the kinetic friction: fk = μk FN = (0.30)(127.3 N) = 38.2 N.
  • You find the net force, 73.5 − 38.2 = 35.3 N, and a = 35.3 / 15 = 2.35 m/s² down the slope.
Problem 5 · Coupled half-Atwood table system

A 4.0 kg cart on a horizontal bench is tethered across a frictionless pulley to a 2.0 kg hanging block. Bench kinetic friction is μk = 0.25. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Use the whole-system method to calculate the acceleration of the coupled assembly.
  2. Isolate the hanging 2.0 kg block to calculate the cord tension T.
  3. Check the tension by isolating the horizontal 4.0 kg cart.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Whole-system acceleration: You find the driving force: Fg,hang = (2.0)(9.8) = 19.6 N.
  • You find the bench friction: fk = (0.25)(4.0)(9.8) = 9.8 N.
  • You find the acceleration: a = (19.6 − 9.8) / (4.0 + 2.0) = 9.8 / 6.0 = 1.63 m/s².
  • (b) Hanging block: You isolate it: m2g − T = m2a.
  • You find the tension: T = 2.0(9.8 − 1.63) = 2.0(8.17) = 16.3 N.
  • (c) Cart check: You isolate the cart: T − fk = m1a.
  • You find the tension: T = 9.8 N + (4.0 kg)(1.63 m/s²) = 9.8 + 6.53 = 16.3 N. The two values match.
