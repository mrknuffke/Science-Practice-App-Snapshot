01Hook
Mission

Level 2: Force Frontier. Skyscraper Hoist Complex, Structural Engineering. When bodies are joined by cables, they accelerate together as one system.

Inside modern skyscrapers, passenger elevators carry thousands of kilograms smoothly up and down hundreds of meters.

The electric motor does not lift the full weight of the elevator cabin directly.

Instead, heavy steel cables loop over an overhead motorized pulley to connect the car to a massive counterweight.

As the elevator car rises, the counterweight descends. The two opposing weights nearly balance each other.

This counterbalance reduces the net driving force. Modest motors can then control immense loads safely.

02 The whole-system method

The Whole-System Method

When objects are joined by taut cords, rods, or direct contact, they move with a shared acceleration magnitude:

a1 = a2 = asys

Coupled problems become straightforward when you use the whole-system method:

Two-Step Analysis Protocol

  1. Step 1: Find System Acceleration. Treat all coupled masses as one single composite body of mass mtotal = m1 + m2. Internal forces like cable tension cancel out:
    a = ΣFexternal / mtotal
  2. Step 2: Find Internal Tension. Isolate one single mass. Draw its individual free body diagram. Apply Newton's Second Law (ΣF = ma) to solve for cord tension:
    T − f = m1a   &implies;   T = m1a + f
Common mistake

Tension does not cancel when you look at one mass alone. It cancels only when you treat the whole system as one body.

Ideal Cord and Pulley Features

Introductory mechanics assumes ideal mechanical hardware to isolate essential physical principles:

  • Massless, Inelastic Cord: Tension is uniform throughout the entire length, and connected bodies share identical acceleration.
  • Frictionless, Massless Pulley: Redirects the force vector without consuming energy or changing tension magnitude.
03 The Atwood machine

The Classical Atwood Machine

In 1784, the English mathematician George Atwood designed an apparatus to measure gravitational acceleration:

Two masses (m1 and m2) hang vertically over a low-friction pulley. Assume m2 > m1.

1. Driving Force & Acceleration:

The heavier mass pulls down while the lighter mass resists:

ΣFext = (m2 − m1)g
a = g × (m2 − m1) / (m1 + m2)
2. Cord Tension Magnitude:

Isolating mass 1 moving upward (T − m1g = m1a):

T = m1(g + a)
T = 2 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2)
Common mistake

Cord tension is not equal to the weight of either mass. It always falls strictly between the two individual weights:

m1g < T < m2g

If tension equaled m2g, the heavier mass could not accelerate downward. If tension equaled m1g, the lighter mass could not rise.

05 Cables

Cable Safety & Acceleration Spikes

Common mistake

The cable does not carry only the weight of the load. When the load speeds up as it rises, the tension is larger than the weight.

In crane and elevator operations, cable failure occurs most often during abrupt upward accelerations.

When a crane lifts a stationary steel beam of mass m, the static cable tension equals:

Tstatic = mg

When the crane accelerates the load upward at rate a, the cable must supply additional force:

Tdynamic = m(g + a)

An upward acceleration equal to 0.5 g increases cable tension by 50 percent above static weight.

Engineers apply a safety factor (typically 5 to 10) to prevent cable rupture under sudden acceleration surges.

Problem 2.5: High-Rise Construction Crane Hoist

Written response3 marks

A construction hoist car of mass 1200 kg is coupled over an overhead crane pulley to a counterweight of 800 kg. An electric winch assists the counterweight by applying an extra downward pull of 1000 N on the counterweight side.

The steel hoist cable has a certified maximum working tension rating of 12,000 N.

Evaluate whether the cable operates within safe load limits using the Evidence → Claim → Reasoning (ECR) framework.

Scoring guide
  • Givens (1 mark): You list mcar = 1200 kg, mcw = 800 kg, Fwinch = 1000 N and Tmax = 12,000 N.
  • Whole system (1 mark): You use the whole-system method to find the net force (2920 N).
  • You find the acceleration, a = 1.46 m/s².
  • Tension (1 mark): You isolate the hoist car to find T ≈ 10,000 N.
  • You say this is within the safe working limit.
Model response (Evidence, Claim, Reasoning)
  • Evidence: Hoist car mass is 1200 kg. Counterweight mass is 800 kg. Assist winch force is 1000 N. Certified safe cable rating is 12,000 N.
  • Claim: Yes, the cable operates within safe limits. Dynamic cable tension is 10,000 N, safely below the 12,000 N rating limit.
  • Reasoning: Total mass: mtotal = 1200 + 800 = 2000 kg. Net force on hoist car: downward gravity (11,760 N) opposes counterweight gravity (7840 N) and winch (1000 N). Net force is 2920 N downward. Car accelerates downward at a = 2920 / 2000 = 1.46 m/s². Cable tension on car: T = 1200(9.8 − 1.46) = 10,008 N ≈ 10,000 N. Because 10,008 N < 12,000 N, the hoist operates safely.
