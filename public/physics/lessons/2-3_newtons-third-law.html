Lesson 2-3 · Newton's Third Law & Action-Reaction

An astronaut is stranded five metres from the airlock. She throws her toolbox away to get home.

Forces never come alone. Every force is an interaction between two bodies. This lesson shows how the two forces of an interaction behave. It also shows what stays the same when two bodies push on each other.

01Hook
Mission

Level 2: Force Frontier. Orbital Space Station, Spacewalk Readings.

Imagine an astronaut floating untethered five meters outside the International Space Station with zero velocity.

She holds a heavy 15-kilogram toolbox. There is nothing to hold onto, and no air to push against.

To return to the airlock, she hurls the toolbox directly away from the station.

As she pushes the toolbox forward, the toolbox pushes her backward toward safety.

You cannot touch something without it touching you back. This symmetry is Newton's Third Law of Motion.

02 Interaction pairs

Interaction Pairs

Newton saw that a force is not a property of one object. A force is an interaction between two objects:

FA on B = −FB on A

Whenever A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.

The Four Features of a Third-Law Pair

  1. Two Different Objects: The forces act on two separate bodies (one on A, one on B).
  2. Same Physical Type: Both forces share the same physical mechanism (both gravitational, both normal, or both magnetic).
  3. Strictly Equal Magnitudes: |FA on B| = |FB on A| at every single instant.
  4. Strictly Opposite Directions: The two vectors point in exactly opposite directions along the line of interaction.

The Table and Book Fallacy

Consider a textbook resting on a table. Downward gravity acts on the book. Upward normal force acts on the book.

Common mistake

Normal force and gravity on a resting book are not a third-law pair. Both forces act on the same object, the book. They balance because of Newton's First Law, not Newton's Third Law.

Each of these forces has its real partner on a different object:

Gravitational Pair:

Earth pulls the book downward with gravitational force Fg.
Reaction: The book pulls the entire Earth upward with identical gravitational force Fg.

Contact Normal Pair:

The table pushes upward on the book with normal force FN.
Reaction: The book pushes downward on the table with identical normal force FN.

03 The horse-and-cart question

Why Action-Reaction Forces Never Cancel

Students often ask: if every force has an equal and opposite reaction force, why does anything accelerate?

This classic question is known as the horse-and-cart paradox.

Common mistake

The Horse's Argument. “If I pull forward on the cart, the cart pulls backward on me with equal force. The two forces sum to zero. Therefore, we cannot move.”

The Physical Resolution:

Forces only cancel when they act on the same object. Action-reaction forces act on different objects.

To determine whether the horse accelerates, draw a free body diagram of the horse alone.

The horse pushes backward against the ground with its hooves. By Newton's Third Law, the ground pushes forward on the horse with static friction.

If the ground's forward push exceeds the cart's backward pull, the horse experiences a net forward force and accelerates:

ΣFhorse = Fground on horse − Fcart on horse > 0
04Conservation of momentum

Momentum and Conservation of Momentum

A moving object is harder to stop when it has more mass or more speed. Momentum measures both. It is the mass times the velocity:

p = mv   (unit: kg · m/s)

Momentum is a vector. In one dimension, its sign shows the direction.

Now take two bodies that push on each other, such as two carts that collide. By Newton's Third Law, the two forces are equal and opposite. They also act for the same time. So each cart's momentum changes by the same amount. The two changes point in opposite directions.

The total momentum is the sum of the two momenta:

ptotal = m1v1 + m2v2

If no net outside force acts on the two bodies, the total momentum stays the same. Scientists call this conservation of momentum:

ptotal, before = ptotal, after

Worked Example: Two Carts

Cart 1 (2.0 kg) moves at +3.0 m/s. Cart 2 (6.0 kg) moves toward it at −2.0 m/s. Take the right as positive.

ptotal, before = 2.0(+3.0) + 6.0(−2.0) = +6.0 − 12.0 = −6.0 kg · m/s.
After the collision, ptotal, after is also −6.0 kg · m/s.

Each cart's momentum changes. The total does not.

Common mistake

Momentum is conserved for the two carts together, not for each cart alone. Each cart's momentum changes in a collision. The total stays the same.

06 Rocket propulsion

Rocket Propulsion in Vacuum

Common mistake

Many students think rockets move by pushing against the surrounding air. If that were true, rockets would fail in the vacuum of outer space. In reality, rockets work better in space because air resistance vanishes.

A rocket accelerates by pushing backward against its own expelled propellant:

Frocket on gas = −Fgas on rocket

High-pressure combustion forces gas molecules out the engine nozzle at supersonic speeds.

By Newton's Third Law, the expelled gas exerts an equal forward thrust force on the rocket body.

Connecting the Second and Third Laws

While contact forces are strictly equal, resulting accelerations depend on mass:

agas = F / mgas          arocket = F / mrocket

Because gas mass is small, gas accelerates backward at extreme rates. Because rocket mass is large, vehicle acceleration is smooth and gradual.

Problem 2.3: Satellite Space Tug Docking

Written response3 marks

An automated orbital space tug with mass 2500 kg docks with an inactive 10,000 kg communication satellite. During the capture maneuver, the tug exerts an average contact push of 5000 N on the satellite.

A flight controller makes a claim: “Because the tug pushes the satellite forward, the tug experiences zero backward force.”

Evaluate this claim using the Evidence → Claim → Reasoning (ECR) framework.

Scoring guide
  • Evidence (1 mark): You list mtug = 2500 kg, msat = 10,000 kg and Ftug on sat = 5000 N.
  • Claim (1 mark): You say the claim is false.
  • You use Newton's Third Law: the satellite pushes back on the tug with 5000 N.
  • Reasoning (1 mark): You calculate atug = −2.0 m/s² and asat = +0.50 m/s².
  • You explain the difference with a = F / m.
Model response (Evidence, Claim, Reasoning)
  • Evidence: Tug mass is 2500 kg. Satellite mass is 10,000 kg. The tug exerts an average forward contact force of 5000 N on the satellite.
  • Claim: The flight controller's claim is false. The satellite exerts an equal and opposite backward force of 5000 N on the tug.
  • Reasoning: By Newton's Third Law, Fsat on tug = −Ftug on sat = −5000 N. By Newton's Second Law, the tug decelerates at a = −5000 N / 2500 kg = −2.0 m/s². The satellite accelerates at a = +5000 N / 10,000 kg = +0.50 m/s². The tug experiences four times greater acceleration because of its smaller mass.
