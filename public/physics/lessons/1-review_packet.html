02Level 1 synthesis · Main ideas

Part 1: Main Ideas Digest

Pillar 1: 1D Position, Vectors & Constant Velocity

Motion occurs relative to a chosen reference frame with an established origin (x = 0) and positive/negative directions.

  • Scalar vs. Vector: Scalars have magnitude only (distance, speed, time, mass). Vectors require both magnitude and spatial direction (+ or − in 1D; displacement, velocity, acceleration).
  • Distance vs. Displacement: Distance (d) is the scalar sum of all ground covered. Displacement (Δx = xf − xi) is the straight-line vector difference between final and initial positions.
  • Average Velocity: v = Δx / Δt. A negative velocity means moving toward the negative coordinates, not slowing down.

Pillar 3: Uniform Acceleration & Kinematics Equations

When acceleration is uniform (constant a), motion is governed by the Big Three Kinematics Equations:

1. vf = vi + a·t (when displacement Δx is not needed)
2. Δx = vi·t + ½·a·t² (when final velocity vf is not needed)
3. vf² = vi² + 2·a·Δx (when elapsed time t is not needed)

Free Fall: An object dropped from rest (vi = 0) accelerates downward solely due to gravity (g = 9.8 m/s²). Fall time is t = √(2·h / g), and vertical distance fallen is Δy = ½·g·t².

Pillar 4: 2D Projectile Motion Independence

Extra depth. The rest of the review works without it.

In horizontal projectile launches (Operation Projectile Precision):

  • Horizontal component (x): Moves at constant velocity (ax = 0, vx stays constant). Range: Δx = vx × t.
  • Vertical component (y): In free fall starting with zero initial vertical velocity (viy = 0, ay = −g).
  • Key Invariant: Horizontal motion has zero effect on how long it takes an object to fall. A bullet fired horizontally hits the ground at the exact same instant as a bullet dropped from the same height.

Part 2: Targeted Remediation Practice Problems

Problem 1 · 1D position and vectors

An automated rover on Mars begins at x = 0 m. It drives +60 meters East in 12 seconds, stops for 4 seconds to drill a rock, and then reverses −20 meters West in 4 seconds.

  1. What is the total distance traveled?
  2. What is the final displacement Δx?
  3. What is the average velocity over the entire 20-second trip?
Scoring guide
  • (a) Distance: You add every leg of the path: 60 m + 0 m + 20 m = 80 meters.
  • (b) Displacement: You subtract: Δx = (+40 m) − (0 m) = +40 meters.
  • (c) Average velocity: You divide: v = (+40 m) / (20 s) = +2.0 m/s.
Problem 2 · Motion graph interpretation

A vehicle moves along a straight test track. Its velocity-time (v-t) graph shows:

  • Segment 1 (t = 0 to 4 s): Velocity increases linearly from 0 m/s to 12 m/s.
  • Segment 2 (t = 4 to 10 s): Velocity stays constant at 12 m/s.
  1. Calculate the acceleration during Segment 1.
  2. Use the area under the curve to calculate the total displacement between t = 0 and t = 10 seconds.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Acceleration: You find the slope: a = (12 m/s − 0 m/s) / (4 s − 0 s) = 3.0 m/s².
  • (b) Displacement: You find the triangle from 0 to 4 s: ½ × (4 s) × (12 m/s) = 24 m.
  • You find the rectangle from 4 to 10 s: (6 s) × (12 m/s) = 72 m.
  • You add the areas: 24 m + 72 m = 96 meters.
Problem 3 · 1D kinematics equation selection

A transport buggy on Planet Telex approaches a supply hangar at 20 m/s. The pilot slams on the emergency brakes. The buggy slows uniformly at −4.0 m/s² until it stops completely (vf = 0).

  1. Which kinematics equation gives the stopping distance without the elapsed time?
  2. Calculate the stopping distance.
Scoring guide
  • (a) Equation: You choose vf² = vi² + 2·a·Δx, because time is not given.
  • (b) Calculation: You substitute: 0² = (20)² + 2(−4.0)·Δx, so 0 = 400 − 8.0·Δx.
  • You solve: 8.0·Δx = 400, so Δx = 50 meters.
Problem 4 · Free fall and cliff drop

A crew calibrates gravity by dropping a metal probe from rest off a cliff 45 meters high. (Use g = 10 m/s² for a quick calculation, or 9.8 m/s² for precision.)

  1. How many seconds does the probe take to strike the ground?
  2. What is its vertical velocity right before impact?
Scoring guide
  • (a) Fall time: You use t = √(2·h / g).
  • With g = 10 m/s²: t = √(2 × 45 / 10) = √9 = 3.0 seconds.
  • With g = 9.8 m/s²: t = √(90 / 9.8) = √9.18 = 3.03 seconds. Both are accepted.
  • (b) Impact velocity: You use vf = vi + g·t = 0 + (10)(3.0) = 30 m/s downward (−30 m/s).
  • With g = 9.8 m/s², the answer is −29.7 m/s.

Problem 5 · 2D horizontal projectile launchEnrichment

Operation Projectile Precision: An aerial supply drone cruises horizontally at an altitude of 20 meters with a steady forward speed of 15 m/s. It releases a supply container to land on an emergency target zone below.

  1. Calculate the time the container takes to reach the ground.
  2. Calculate the horizontal distance (Δx) the container travels while falling. How far before the target must the drone release the container?
Scoring guide
  • (a) Fall time: You use only the vertical motion: t = √(2 × 20 / 9.8) ≈ 2.02 seconds (2.0 s with g = 10 m/s²).
  • (b) Horizontal range: You use the horizontal motion: Δx = vx × t = (15 m/s) × (2.02 s) = 30.3 meters (30.0 m with g = 10 m/s²).
  • You say the drone must release the container 30 meters before it flies over the target.
