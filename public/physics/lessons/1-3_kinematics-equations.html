02The three equations

The Big Three Kinematics Equations

In Lesson 1-2, you learned that constant acceleration yields a linear velocity-time graph. Its slope gives acceleration (a) and its area gives displacement (Δx). Combining these two geometric relationships produces the Big Three Kinematics Equations.

These equations describe any motion with constant acceleration using five core variables:

vi

Initial Velocity (m/s)

vf

Final Velocity (m/s)

a

Acceleration (m/s²)

Δx

Displacement (m)

t

Time Interval (s)

The Equation Matrix

Equation 1: Velocity as a Function of Time

Missing: Δx

vf = vi + at

Use when displacement is neither known nor requested. Direct rearrangement of the definition of acceleration: a = (vf − vi) / t.

Equation 2: Position as a Function of Time

Missing: vf

Δx = vit + ½at²

The cornerstone of projectile and drop calculations. The first term represents displacement if speed were constant; the second term accounts for the velocity boost from acceleration.

Equation 3: The Timeless Equation

Missing: t

vf² = vi² + 2aΔx

Derived by solving Equation 1 for time (t = (vf − vi) / a) and substituting into Equation 2. Essential whenever elapsed time is unknown.

A rover drives at 12 m/s on level ground. It brakes with a constant acceleration of −3.0 m/s² until it stops.

  1. Calculate the time the rover takes to stop.
  2. Calculate the stopping distance.
  3. Calculate how far the rover travels in the first 2.0 s of braking.
Scoring guide
  • Part (a) (1 mark): You use vf = vi + at: 0 = 12 − 3.0t, so t = 4.0 s.
  • Part (b) (1 mark): You use vf² = vi² + 2aΔx: 0 = 144 − 6.0Δx, so Δx = 24 m.
  • Part (c) (2 marks): You use Δx = vit + ½at² and keep a negative.
  • You calculate 12(2.0) + ½(−3.0)(2.0)² = 24 − 6.0 = 18 m.

Perpendicular Independence & Horizontal Launches

The master key to analyzing two-dimensional motion was discovered by Galileo Galilei: perpendicular motions are completely independent of one another. An object moving simultaneously in the horizontal (x) and vertical (y) directions does not experience any cross-talk between coordinates.

Horizontal Dimension (X-Axis)

In projectile flight (ignoring air resistance), gravity acts exclusively downward. No force acts horizontally.

  • Horizontal Acceleration: ax = 0
  • Horizontal Velocity: vx = vx0 = constant
  • Horizontal Displacement: Δx = vx0 · t

Vertical Dimension (Y-Axis)

Gravity exerts a continuous downward force causing uniform downward acceleration.

  • Vertical Acceleration: ay = −g = −9.8 m/s²
  • Initial Vertical Speed (Horizontal Launch): vy0 = 0
  • Vertical Fall: Δy = −½gt²

Deriving Flight Time & Horizontal Range

Because time (t) is a scalar quantity, it is the sole bridge linking the horizontal and vertical worlds. We solve for flight time from the vertical drop height, then substitute into the horizontal equation:

Step 1: Vertical Hang-Time (tflight)

Δy = −½gt²  ⇒  −h = −½gt²  ⇒  tflight = √(2h / g)

Notice that launch speed vx0 does not appear anywhere in this formula. Flight time is determined strictly by cliff height and gravity.

Step 2: Horizontal Range (Δx)

Δx = vx0 · tflight = vx0 √(2h / g)

This combined relationship allows our ISA flight crew to hit any target coordinate with mathematical certainty.

Elysium Canyon Drone Rescue Challenge4 marksEnrichment

A reconnaissance rover drives off a horizontal 20.0-meter cliff ledge on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²) at a forward speed of 8.50 m/s.

  1. Calculate the time required for the rover to hit the canyon floor.
  2. Determine the horizontal distance from the base of the cliff to the impact site.
  3. Calculate the magnitude of the rover's total velocity vector just before impact.
Scoring guide
  • Part (a) [1 Mark]:
    t = √(2h / g) = √[2(20.0 m) / (9.8 m/s²)] = √4.082 = 2.02 s
    (Accept 2.00 s if using g = 10 m/s²).
  • Part (b) [1 Mark]:
    Δx = vx0 · t = (8.50 m/s)(2.02 s) = 17.2 m
    (Accept 17.0 m if using g = 10 m/s²).
  • Part (c) [2 Marks]:
    vy = vy0 − gt = 0 − (9.8 m/s²)(2.02 s) = −19.8 m/s
    vtotal = √(vx² + vy²) = √[(8.50)² + (−19.8)²] = √[72.25 + 392.0] = 21.5 m/s
    1 mark for finding vertical velocity component; 1 mark for vector Pythagorean hypotenuse (accept 21.5 to 21.8 m/s).
