02 Reference frames

Reference Frames, Position, and Vectors

Motion is relative. If you sit quietly on an MRT train moving at 80 km/h, are you moving? Relative to the passenger beside you, your position does not change at all. Relative to someone standing on the platform at Woodlands station, you are hurtling forward at highway speed.

Every physics problem begins by defining a reference frame. This agreed coordinate system establishes an origin point (position zero) and positive and negative directions.

Scalars versus Vectors

In physics, quantities divide into two distinct species:

  • Scalars: Quantities that have magnitude (size and units) but no spatial direction. Examples include time (12 s), mass (45 kg), temperature (295 K), and distance (100 m).
  • Vectors: Quantities that require both a magnitude and a specific direction. In one dimension, direction is communicated simply by a positive (+) or negative (−) sign. Examples include position (+15 m), displacement (−30 m), and velocity (+4.5 m/s).

Distance versus Displacement

Distance and displacement measure different quantities:

Common mistake

Many students treat distance and displacement as the same thing. Distance counts every meter the object travels. Displacement compares only the start and the end.

Displacement (Δx) is the straight-line vector difference between your final position (xf) and your initial position (xi):

Δx = xf − xi

03 Constant velocity

Uniform Motion & Average Velocity

When an object travels with uniform motion, it covers equal displacements in equal time intervals. In this state, its speed and direction do not change. Its acceleration is zero.

We define average velocity (v or v) as the total displacement divided by the elapsed time interval:

v = ΔxΔt = xf − xitf − ti

Notice the critical consequence of direction:

  • If an object moves in the positive direction (Δx > 0), its velocity is positive (+v).
  • If an object moves in the negative direction (Δx < 0), its velocity is negative (−v).
  • If an object makes a round trip and returns to its starting point, its net displacement is zero (Δx = 0). Its average velocity is 0 m/s, no matter how far it went.
Common mistake

A negative velocity does not mean the object is slowing down. It means the object moves in the negative direction.

Speed versus Velocity

Average speed is distance divided by time. It has no sign and is always positive. Average velocity is displacement divided by time and carries directional meaning:

The Round-Trip Comparison

Eliud Kipchoge runs a complete 400-meter oval track in 50 seconds. His average speed is (400 m) / (50 s) = 8.0 m/s. But because his start and finish lines coincide, his displacement is 0 meters. His average velocity for the lap is 0.0 m/s.

Problem 1.3: Highway Transit along the Bukit Timah Expressway (BKE)

Written response3 marks

You drive from the Singapore American School campus in Woodlands to the base of Bukit Timah Hill. The highway displacement along the expressway is 10,000 meters south (+10,000 m). The drive takes 1,200 seconds (20 minutes).

A passenger states: "We traveled 10,000 m in 1,200 s, which equals 8.33 m/s. Therefore, our speedometer stayed at 8.33 m/s the entire way."

Evaluate this statement using the Evidence → Claim → Reasoning (ECR) framework.

  • Evidence: State the quantitative data: total displacement (Δx = 10,000 m) and total time (Δt = 1,200 s). Note road observations such as stoplights, merging traffic, and ramps.
  • Claim: Refute the passenger: 8.33 m/s represents the average velocity across the 20-minute trip. It does not mean instantaneous speed stayed constant at every point.
  • Reasoning: Connect the evidence to definitions: average velocity is an aggregate ratio (v = Δx / Δt). In real travel, the vehicle accelerates, cruises, and slows for ramps. Instantaneous velocity fluctuates while maintaining an average of 8.33 m/s.
Scoring guide
  • Average velocity (1 mark): You calculate v = 10,000 m / 1,200 s = 8.33 m/s (or 30 km/h).
  • Average and instantaneous (1 mark): You say average velocity is total displacement over total time.
  • You say a speedometer shows the velocity at one moment.
  • Evidence (1 mark): You give a real reason the speed changes: starts, cruising, traffic.
  • You say the average stays 8.33 m/s while the instant speed varies.
