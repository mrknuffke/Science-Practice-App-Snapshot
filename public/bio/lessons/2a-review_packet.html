02Unit 2a synthesis · Main ideas

Part 1: Main ideas digest

Pillar 1: Photosynthesis stores energy (HS-LS1-5)

Carbon Dioxide + Water + Light Energy → Glucose + Oxygen

A producer's matter comes from carbon dioxide and water. Its energy comes from light. Photosynthesis stores that light energy as chemical energy in sugar.

Producers also respire, day and night, to power their own cells. In daylight, photosynthesis runs faster, so they take in more carbon dioxide than they give off.

In 2023, people added 11.1 billion tonnes of carbon to the air. The ocean took up 2.9 and the land 2.3, so 5.9 stayed. The air's store grows whenever more carbon flows in than flows out.

The air's yearly breath

Problem 1 · Photosynthesis and respiration4 marks

Scientists have measured carbon dioxide on Mauna Loa, a volcano in Hawaii, since 1958. The table shows the monthly averages for 2023, and one value from 2024.

Carbon dioxide at Mauna Loa, Hawaii
MonthCarbon dioxide (ppm)
January 2023419.5
March 2023421.0
May 2023424.0
July 2023421.8
September 2023418.5
November 2023420.5
January 2024422.8

Source: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, Mauna Loa monthly mean carbon dioxide. Mauna Loa is in the Northern Hemisphere, where summer runs from June to August.

(a) Between which two months in the table does carbon dioxide fall the most? By how much does it fall?
(b) Use photosynthesis to explain the fall.
(c) Use respiration to explain why carbon dioxide rises again from September to May.
(d) Calculate the rise from January 2023 to January 2024, and explain why the air does not return to where it started.

Scoring guide
  • (a) Reading the table (1 mark): From May to September, 424.0 minus 418.5 is a fall of 5.5 ppm.
  • (b) Photosynthesis (1 mark): You explain that northern plants grow fast in summer, and photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air faster than respiration returns it.
  • (c) Respiration (1 mark): You explain that in autumn and winter, photosynthesis slows, but respiration and decay go on, so carbon dioxide builds up.
  • (d) The yearly rise (1 mark): 422.8 minus 419.5 is a rise of about 3.3 ppm.
  • You explain that people add more carbon each year than the ocean and land take up, so the store keeps growing.

The same scientists measure carbon dioxide in other places too. Utqiaġvik is a town in far northern Alaska. The third station sits at the South Pole, in Antarctica.

Source: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, monthly in-situ carbon dioxide for Utqiaġvik (Barrow), Mauna Loa and the South Pole. At the South Pole, carbon dioxide rose a little every month of 2023, with no seasonal fall. Most of Earth's land lies north of the equator.

(a) Which station has the largest seasonal swing?
(b) Explain why the swing is largest there. Use photosynthesis and respiration.
(c) Explain why the South Pole shows no summer dip.
(d) Draw a model with the atmosphere and the biosphere as boxes. Add one labelled arrow for summer and one for winter, and show which is larger in each season.

Scoring guide
  • (a) Reading the table (1 mark): Utqiaġvik, with a swing of 18.0 ppm.
  • (b) The far north (1 mark): You explain that there is a lot of land with producers in the north. In summer, their photosynthesis pulls the air's carbon dioxide down hard, and in winter respiration returns it.
  • (c) The South Pole (1 mark): You explain that there are few land producers near the South Pole, so there is little seasonal photosynthesis to change the air there.
  • (d) Model (1 mark): A photosynthesis arrow runs from the atmosphere to the biosphere, and a respiration arrow runs back.
  • Your model shows photosynthesis larger in summer and respiration larger in winter.
