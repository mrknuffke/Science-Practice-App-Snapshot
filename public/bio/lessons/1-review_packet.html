Part 1: Main ideas digest

Pillar 1: Cellular respiration and energy transfer (HS-LS1-7)

Cellular respiration is a chemical process. The cell breaks the bonds of glucose and oxygen and forms new, lower-energy bonds in carbon dioxide and water. This transfers energy to ATP and to heat.

C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + ATP + heat
  • Matter tracking: The carbon and oxygen atoms from glucose leave the body as carbon dioxide in your breath. The hydrogen atoms from glucose join oxygen from your breath to make water.
  • Energy accounting: Mitochondria do not create energy. Energy stored in the bonds of glucose moves to ATP, and ATP powers cell work such as muscle contraction.

Pillar 2: The parts of a negative feedback loop (HS-LS1-3)

Homeostasis keeps internal variables near a set point. Every negative feedback loop has the same parts:

The five parts of a negative feedback loop
ComponentRole in homeostatic controlExample (thermoregulation)
StimulusA physical or chemical change that moves a variable away from its set pointFreezing air drops body core temperature
Receptor (sensor)Cells or nerve endings that monitor the variableTemperature sensors in the skin and in blood vessels
Control centerCompares sensor signals with the set point and commands actionThe hypothalamus
EffectorCells, tissues, or organs that carry out the corrective responseSkin blood vessels (narrow), skeletal muscles (shiver)
ResponseAn action that opposes the stimulus and restores balanceHeat loss falls, heat production rises, core temperature returns to 37°C

Matter conservation and atom accounting

Problem 1 · Cellular respiration3 marks

During a marathon, a runner metabolizes 360 grams (2.0 moles) of glucose.
(a) Write the balanced chemical equation for the complete aerobic cellular respiration of 1 mole of glucose.
(b) Count the oxygen atoms on each side of the equation. Show that they match.
(c) Where do the six carbon atoms in one glucose molecule end up?

Scoring guide
  • (a) Equation (1 mark): C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + ATP + heat.
  • (b) Oxygen count (1 mark): You count 18 oxygen atoms in: 6 in glucose and 12 in six O2.
  • You count 18 out: 12 in six CO2 and 6 in six H2O.
  • (c) Carbon (1 mark): You say all six carbon atoms leave in six CO2 molecules.
  • You say she breathes them out.

Water balance without ADH

Problem 3 · Osmoregulation and ADH3 marks

Alcohol stops the pituitary gland from releasing ADH. Explain why a person who drinks a lot of alcohol becomes dehydrated and makes a large volume of urine, even when the drinks add liquid.

Scoring guide
  • Hormone (1 mark): You say alcohol stops the pituitary from releasing ADH.
  • You say ADH in the blood then falls.
  • Kidney effect (1 mark): You say that without ADH the kidney tubes return less water to the blood.
  • Outcome (1 mark): You say the kidneys pass a large volume of dilute urine.
  • You say the body loses water and becomes dehydrated.

The marathon runner's collapse

Problem 5 · Summative anchor challenge4 marks

At kilometer 38 of a marathon in 31°C heat and high humidity, an elite runner staggers and collapses. Medical staff record:

  • Core body temperature: 39.8 °C (severe overheating)
  • Blood concentration: 312 mOsm/kg (severe dehydration)
  • Blood glucose: 52 mg/dL (severe low blood sugar)
  • Skin: hot, flushed, and dry (sweating has stopped)

Task: Write an Evidence-Claim-Reasoning argument that explains how all three feedback loops failing at once led to collapse.

Scoring guide
  • Claim (1 mark): You say all three loops failed together.
  • You say the brain ran short of fuel and blood pressure fell.
  • Evidence (1 mark): You cite 39.8°C, 312 mOsm/kg, 52 mg/dL, and the end of sweating.
  • Water and heat (1 mark): You say heavy sweating raised blood concentration and lowered blood volume.
  • You say sweating then stopped to protect blood pressure, so heat built up.
  • Fuel (1 mark): You say long exercise used up the liver's glycogen.
  • You say glucagon then had nothing to release, so blood glucose fell and the brain went without fuel.
