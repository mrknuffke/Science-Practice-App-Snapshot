Lesson 5-4a · Electromagnetic Radiation & Matter

A 1,000-watt microwave oven heats soup. A 0.001-watt dental X-ray needs a lead apron.

We live in a bath of invisible waves. Radio carries signals to your phone, microwaves warm food, and ultraviolet light from the Sun can burn your skin. The word “radiation” often sounds like danger. But power is not what makes some radiation harmful. The energy of each single photon decides. This page shows how to judge a claim about radiation.

  • What each band does. Microwaves make molecules rotate. Infrared makes bonds vibrate. Visible light moves electrons. Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays can break bonds.

01Energy in packets

Light travels as photons, and higher frequency means more energy per photon.

All electromagnetic radiation moves through space at the speed of light. It acts as a wave and as a stream of tiny packets of energy. Each packet is a photon.

A photon’s energy depends on its frequency. A high-frequency photon has more energy than a low-frequency photon. Frequency and wavelength trade off, so a short wavelength means a high frequency.

The one idea to hold onto

The energy of one photon depends only on its frequency. A brighter source sends more photons. It does not make any single photon stronger.

Common mistake

Thinking a brighter or more powerful source has stronger photons. Power counts all the photons together. Each photon still carries the energy of its own frequency.

02Radiation meets matter

What a photon does to matter depends on its energy.

When radiation strikes matter, the matter absorbs the photons. What happens next depends on the energy of each photon.

→

1. Microwaves make molecules rotate

Microwave photons have little energy. They twist polar molecules such as water back and forth. The twisting turns into heat as molecules rub, and that warms your food. These photons cannot break a bond.

→

2. Infrared makes bonds vibrate

Infrared photons make bonds stretch and bend. This is how greenhouse gases such as CO2, CH4 and H2O absorb heat that Earth radiates.

→

3. Visible light moves electrons

Visible photons lift outer electrons to higher energy levels. In plants, this drives photosynthesis. In your retina, it starts the signal for vision.

→

4. Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays can ionize

These photons carry enough energy to knock electrons off atoms and to break bonds. This is ionizing radiation. In living cells it can break DNA, which can cause mutations, cell death and cancer.

Common mistake

Thinking all radiation damages cells. Radio waves, microwaves, infrared and visible light cannot break bonds. A strong source can still heat and burn tissue, but it does not ionize it.

04Scientific literacy

To judge a claim about radiation, ask about the photons.

Claims that Wi-Fi, 5G towers or cell phones cause cancer spread widely online. How does a scientist judge a claim like this in a published source?

Ask three questions. Which band does the source use? Does one photon carry enough energy to break a bond? Does the claim confuse power with photon energy?

The one idea to hold onto

A bond cannot save up energy from many weak photons. One photon breaks one bond, or none. A radio tower sends huge numbers of photons, but every one of them is too weak to break a DNA bond.

A strong enough transmitter can warm tissue and even burn it. That is a heating effect, and it is different from damage to DNA.

Common mistake

Thinking a stronger transmitter must be more dangerous to DNA. Power sets how much heat a source makes. Photon energy sets whether a bond can break.

Calculating photon energy, and what happens to DNA

Extra depth. The rest of the lesson works without it.

Photon energy

Light of frequency f and wavelength λ travels at the speed of light c. A photon’s energy E follows from Planck’s constant h = 6.626 × 10−34 J·s.

c = λ f   E = h f = h c / λ

Scientists often give photon energy in electron-volts (eV). One eV is a tiny unit of energy. A typical covalent bond in DNA needs about 3 to 4 eV to break. Photons above about 10 eV can ionize atoms.

A 5G photon at 28 GHz carries 0.00012 eV. That is about 30,000 times too weak to break a DNA bond.

What ionizing radiation does to DNA

An ionizing photon can break the covalent bonds of the DNA backbone. This can cut both strands. It also makes hydroxyl free radicals, which attack nearby molecules. Cells that repair this damage wrongly can mutate.

07Explain it to yourself

An advertisement says: “5G towers give off dangerous radiation that breaks the DNA in your brain cells. Buy our sticker to block it.”

Name the band that 5G uses. Say what one photon of that band can and cannot do. Write your answer first, then compare it with the model answer.

One way to say it

5G uses radio waves. Radio photons carry far too little energy to break a bond in DNA. Radio is non-ionizing radiation.

The advertisement may be thinking about power. But a bond cannot save up energy from many weak photons, so a stronger signal does not change the result. At most, a strong source warms tissue. The claim is not supported.

Why this matters

Mineral sunscreens contain tiny particles of zinc oxide or titanium dioxide. The particles absorb ultraviolet photons before they reach your skin. They turn the photon energy into harmless heat, so the DNA below stays whole.
