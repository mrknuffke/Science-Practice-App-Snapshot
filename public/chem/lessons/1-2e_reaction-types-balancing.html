Lesson 1-2e · Reaction Types & Balancing

Keep the atoms, balance the reaction.

A chemical reaction does not create atoms. It remodels them. Bonds break and new bonds form, but the building blocks never vanish. The Law of Conservation of Mass says every atom present before a reaction is still there afterward. You show this by balancing the equation with coefficients. By the end, you can sort reactions into five patterns and balance an equation.

Before: Two solid sodium atoms and one diatomic chlorine gas molecule ready to collide.
Rearrangement: Each sodium transfers one electron to a chlorine atom, forming Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.
After: Opposite ions lock into a stable 2 NaCl crystal lattice with 100% atom conservation.

Scope
Synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement and combustion. Acid-base reactions are recognized as double replacement. Balancing changes coefficients, never subscripts.

Core Claims

  • Reaction types: Reactions fall into five patterns: synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement and combustion. An acid-base reaction is a double replacement.
  • Law of conservation: Atoms are never created or destroyed. Every atom in the reactants appears in the products.
  • Balancing rules: Change coefficients to level the atom count. Never change a subscript, because it defines the substance.

01Patterns of change

Five blueprints of rearrangement.

Chemists do not memorize millions of reactions. They sort reactions into a few blueprints. Look at how the reactants combine and you can predict what kind of products form.

Five reaction blueprints

Synthesis

A + B → AB

Example: 2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO

Signature: Two things go in and one thing comes out. Elements or simple compounds combine into a single compound.

Decomposition

AB → A + B

Example: 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂

Signature: One reactant, several products. It usually needs heat, electricity or a spark to break it apart.

Single Replacement

A + BC → AC + B

Example: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Signature: A lone element sits on each side of the arrow. One element pushes another out of its compound and takes its place.

Double Replacement

AB + CD → AD + CB

Example: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Signature: Two compounds swap partners. Each positive ion pairs with the other negative ion. A solid precipitate often drops out of solution.

Combustion

fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Example: CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O

Signature: O₂ is on the left. CO₂ and H₂O are on the right. If those three appear and heat is released, the reaction is combustion.

Acid-Base Neutralization

acid + base → salt + water

Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Signature: An acid (written with H first) meets a base (written with -OH). Water and an ionic salt form.

The name "neutralization" is beyond this course's assessment boundary. You will not be asked to classify a reaction as neutralization. Recognize it as a double replacement.

Common mistake

Hunting for a lone element in every reaction. If both reactants are compounds, the reaction cannot be single replacement. The two compounds swap partners, so it is double replacement.

02The rule

Adjust coefficients, lock the subscripts.

To keep the atoms equal, you might want to change a subscript inside a formula. Never do this. Subscripts define the chemical identity of a substance. Change H2O to H2O2 and you model hydrogen peroxide, a bleach, instead of water. You cannot change the ingredients to balance the scale.

Instead, change the coefficients, the numbers in front of formulas. A coefficient multiplies the whole molecule. A 2 in front of H2O (2 H2O) means two separate water molecules. That doubles the hydrogen and oxygen atoms and keeps the identity.

Walkthrough: balancing methane combustion

The one idea to hold onto

Think of each compound as a sealed plastic box. You can add more boxes (coefficients). You cannot open a box and change the atoms inside (subscripts).

Common mistake

Changing a subscript to balance an equation. H2O to H4O matches the hydrogens, but it names a substance that is not water.
