Lesson 5-1a · The Life & Energy of the Sun

The Sun fuses 600 million tons of hydrogen every second. It has stayed the same size for 4.6 billion years.

Every second, the Sun fuses 600 million metric tons of hydrogen into helium. That releases enormous energy. Yet the Sun neither blows apart nor collapses. Gravity pulls it in, pressure pushes it out, and the two stay equal. This page shows how that balance works and how the energy reaches Earth.

01Why the Sun stays one size

The Sun does not blow apart, and it does not collapse.

The Sun has about 333,000 times the mass of Earth, or 1.989 × 1030 kg. All of that mass pulls inward. Why does gravity not crush the Sun into a black hole?

The core also makes huge amounts of energy. Why does that energy not blow the Sun to pieces?

The answer is hydrostatic equilibrium: two pushes that stay equal.

↓

1. Gravity pulls in

Every part of the Sun pulls on every other part. The pull squeezes the Sun toward its center. In the core, the squeeze raises the pressure to 250 billion atmospheres.

↑

2. Pressure pushes out

The core is at 15,000,000 °C. Hydrogen nuclei fuse there and release energy. The hot gas pushes outward, and that push matches the pull of gravity.

The one idea to hold onto

The Sun is its own thermostat. If the core cools, gravity squeezes it and the squeeze heats it again. If the core overheats, it expands and cools. Either way, the Sun returns to the same size.

Common mistake

Thinking the Sun is a bomb that only luck holds together. Fusion pushes out all the time and gravity pulls in all the time. The two stay equal, so the Sun does not explode and does not collapse.

02Where the energy comes from

Fusion in the core powers the Sun.

In the Sun’s core, hydrogen nuclei join to make helium nuclei. This is nuclear fusion. Each joining releases energy.

The Sun fuses about 600 million metric tons of hydrogen every second. That energy keeps the core hot, and the hot core keeps the pressure high.

Common mistake

Thinking the Sun burns like a campfire. Burning is a chemical reaction that rearranges atoms. Fusion changes the nuclei themselves, and it releases far more energy for each atom.

Enrichment

The proton-proton chain and mass defect

Extra depth. The rest of the lesson works without it.

In the Sun, four hydrogen nuclei become one helium-4 nucleus in three steps. This is the proton-proton chain. Each step releases energy.

Mass defect

Weigh the four hydrogen nuclei (protons) and the helium-4 nucleus they make. The helium nucleus is lighter.

  • Four separate protons: 4 × 1.007276 u = 4.029104 u
  • One helium-4 nucleus: 4.001506 u
  • Missing mass, called the mass defect: 0.027598 u (0.71%)

The missing mass is not lost. It becomes energy, as Einstein’s equation says:

E = Δm · c2

The speed of light squared is huge: c2 ≈ 9 × 1016 m2/s2. Turning 1.0 gram of mass into energy releases 90 trillion joules, the same as burning about 3,500 tons of coal. In the Sun, 4.3 million tons of mass become energy every second.

03From the core to Earth

Energy takes 100,000 years to leave the Sun and 8 minutes to reach Earth.

Light made in the core cannot go straight out. The core is 150 times denser than liquid water. Light travels only 0.1 millimeters before it hits a particle and bounces off in a random direction.

→

1. The inner 70%: a slow zigzag

Light is absorbed and sent out again trillions of times. It zigzags outward for 100,000 to 170,000 years. Each time, it loses a little energy. Gamma rays from the core become visible, infrared and ultraviolet light.

→

2. The outer 30%, then space

Rising gas carries the energy up, like a pot of boiling oatmeal. At the surface (5,778 K), light escapes into space. It crosses 150 million kilometers to Earth in 8 minutes and 20 seconds.

Common mistake

Thinking the sunlight on your face left the core a few minutes ago. The trip across space takes 8 minutes. The trip out of the Sun takes about 100,000 years.

04Evidence from other stars

A star with more mass is brighter and lives a shorter time.

Astronomers measure the mass, brightness and lifetime of many stars. They find a pattern. The more mass a star has, the brighter it is and the shorter it lives. The Sun, at 10 billion years, sits in the middle.

More mass means more weight pressing on the core. The core must be hotter to hold that weight up. A hotter core fuses hydrogen much faster.

Lifetime depends on the fuel and on how fast the star burns it. A star with twice the Sun’s mass has twice the fuel. It is 16 times as bright, so it burns that fuel 16 times as fast.

Common mistake

Thinking a star with more fuel lives longer. A star with more mass burns its fuel so much faster that it lives a shorter time.

07Explain it to yourself

A student says: “Fusion makes huge amounts of energy in the Sun’s core. A bomb does the same, and a bomb explodes. The Sun should explode.”

Explain why the Sun does not explode. Use the two pushes.

Say what happens to the core if fusion speeds up. Write your answer first, then compare it with the model answer.

One way to say it

A bomb has nothing holding it together. The Sun has its own gravity. Gravity pulls the gas inward, and the hot gas pushes outward. The two pushes stay equal.

If fusion speeds up, the core gets hotter and pushes harder than gravity pulls. The core expands. Expanding cools the gas and slows fusion. The push falls until it equals gravity again.

  • Lifetime (1 mark): You find 10 billion years × 2 ÷ 16 = 1.25 billion years.
  • Reason (1 mark): You say Zeta burns fuel 16 times as fast, so extra fuel gives no longer life.
Why this matters

Engineers want to copy the Sun’s fusion on Earth, in magnetic reactors such as ITER and in laser facilities such as the National Ignition Facility. Earth has no crushing gravity, so these reactors must heat deuterium-tritium fuel above 100 million °C, six times hotter than the solar core. If they succeed, fusion could make carbon-free electricity from seawater.
