Logarithmic pH & pOH Relationships
pH DefinitionpH = -log₁₀[H⁺] (or -log₁₀[H₃O⁺])
pOH DefinitionpOH = -log₁₀[OH⁻]
Water Autoionization (Kw at 25 °C)[H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴  |  pH + pOH = 14.00
Concentration Inverses[H⁺] = 10^(-pH)  |  [OH⁻] = 10^(-pOH)
Weak Acid & Weak Base Equilibria
Acid Dissociation Constant (Ka)HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻  |  Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻] / [HA]
Approximation Formula[H⁺] ≈ √(Ka × [HA]°) when percent ionization < 5%
Percent IonizationPercent Ionization = ([H⁺]_equilibrium / [HA]°) × 100%
Common Ka Values at 25 °CAcetic acid (CH₃COOH): 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ | Formic acid (HCOOH): 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ | Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃): 4.3 × 10⁻⁷
Titrations & Buffer Systems
Neutralization EquivalenceMoles H⁺ from acid = Moles OH⁻ from base (Ma × Va = Mb × Vb for monoprotic)
Henderson-Hasselbalch EquationpH = pKa + log₁₀([Conjugate Base A⁻] / [Weak Acid HA])
Equivalence Point pHStrong Acid + Strong Base: pH = 7.00. Weak Acid + Strong Base: pH > 7.00 (basic salt).
Storyline Anchor: Ocean Acidification & Calcification
Atmospheric Carbon UptakeCO₂(g) dissolves in oceans forming carbonic acid: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
Proton Neutralization of CarbonateExcess H⁺ protonates carbonate ions: H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ ⇌ HCO₃⁻, lowering carbonate saturation required for shell formation.
