Classification of Chemical Reactions
Synthesis (Combination)Two or more simple reactants combine into one compound: A + B → AB
DecompositionA single compound breaks down into two or more products: AB → A + B
Single ReplacementA free element displaces an element in a compound: A + BC → AC + B
Double ReplacementTwo aqueous ionic compounds exchange ions: AB + CD → AD + CB
CombustionHydrocarbon reacts rapidly with O₂ to yield CO₂ and H₂O plus heat
Stoichiometric Roadmap (Mass to Mass Conversions)
Step 1: Grams A → Moles AMoles A = Mass A (g) / Molar Mass A (g/mol)
Step 2: Moles A → Moles BMoles B = Moles A × (Coefficient B / Coefficient A)
Step 3: Moles B → Grams BMass B = Moles B × Molar Mass B (g/mol)
Particles → MolesMoles = Particles / (6.022 × 10²³ particles/mol)
Limiting Reactant & Yield Formulas
Limiting ReactantThe reactant that produces the smaller theoretical yield of product; completely consumed first.
Excess ReactantThe reactant present in greater quantity than required; some remains unreacted.
Theoretical YieldThe maximum mass of product calculated from the limiting reactant.
Percent YieldPercent Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100%
Storyline Anchor: Cooking & Baking
Chef Recipe AnalogyCoefficients represent recipe proportions (e.g. 2 eggs + 1 cup flour). The ingredient that runs out first limits the total number of baked batches.
Why Yield < 100%Spillage, batter left in bowls, incomplete reaction equilibrium, and side reactions reduce actual recovery.
