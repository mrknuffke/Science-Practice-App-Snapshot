Solution Concentrations & Dilutions
Molarity (M)M = moles of solute / liters of solution (mol/L)
Dilution FormulaM₁ × V₁ = M₂ × V₂ (moles of solute remain constant)
ElectrolytesSoluble ionic compounds dissociate into mobile solvated ions that conduct electricity.
Chemical Equilibrium Law
General ReactionaA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
Equilibrium ExpressionK_eq = [C]^c × [D]^d / ([A]^a × [B]^b)
Excluded SpeciesPure solids (s) and pure liquids (l) have constant activity and are omitted from K_eq.
Magnitude of KK > 1: Products favored at equilibrium; K < 1: Reactants favored at equilibrium.
Reaction Quotient & Le Chatelier's Principle
Q < KReaction proceeds forward (right) to produce more products.
Q > KReaction proceeds in reverse (left) to produce more reactants.
Concentration StressAdding species shifts away from it; removing species shifts toward it.
Pressure / Volume StressDecreasing volume (increasing pressure) shifts toward side with fewer gas moles.
Temperature StressExothermic (ΔH < 0): Heat is a product; heating shifts left, lowering K. Endothermic: Heating shifts right, increasing K.

Carbonate DepletionH⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) ⇌ HCO₃⁻(aq), removing CO₃²⁻ required for shell calcification: Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) ⇌ CaCO₃(s)
